## 1. What this is, and the one hard constraint

A browser-based, two-player co-op elemental puzzle platformer:

- **Sol** (sun) and **Luna** (moon).
- Sun pools kill Luna, moon pools kill Sol, shade pools kill both.
- Each player collects their own gems and stands at their own door to finish a level.
- **Local mode:** both players on one keyboard. Sol uses arrows, Luna uses WASD. Gamepads work.
- **Online mode:** a host creates a room and gets a 5-letter code or a `/<CODE>` invite link. A friend joins in the browser.

The user originally asked for a faithful clone of *Fireboy and Watergirl 1*. That was declined.
The game is proprietary (not open source, despite the user's claim), and the session's rules
forbid recreating a specific existing game's name, characters, art, levels or look.

The user accepted an **original game "heavily inspired by" the genre**. Their stated requirements:

- Sun & Moon theme.
- Online play plus in-person (same keyboard) play.
- Render free tier.
- New GitHub repo.
- The same number of levels as the original (32).

**Keep it original.** Do not add assets, level layouts, names or characters copied from FBWG.
Genre mechanics (elemental pools, plates, levers, lifts, crates) are fine.

public/index.html    Menus (DOM overlays) + canvas. The title card and lobby have a versus row.
public/style.css

No build step and no bundler. The browser loads ES modules directly, and `/shared/*` is served by
the Node server from the `shared/` folder. The only dependency is `ws`.

### Simulation (`shared/sim.js`)

- **World size:** tile `TS=24`, grid `40x30`, world `960x720` logical px. Fixed tick at 60 Hz.
- **Tile codes:** `0` empty, `1` solid, `2` `/` ramp up-right, `3` `\` ramp up-left,
  `4/5/6` sun/moon/shade pool.
- **Pools** are solid tiles whose top is sunk by `POOL_DEPTH=10`.
- **Death check** (`checkHazards`): the player's feet (inset 5px each side) are over a hostile pool
  and the bottom has sunk at least 3px.
- **Physics constants** live in `PHYS` (top of file). Gravity was lowered on Nov 17, 2026 from
  0.42 to 0.33 and `maxFall` from 9.5 to 8.5; `jumpVel` stays 8.25. `node tools/calibrate.js`
  prints the table. Measured now:
  - A jump rises 99 px, 4.1 tiles (was 3.2), apex after 0.40 s.
  - A running jump lasts 0.82 s (was 0.65) and travels 6.6 tiles.
  - Tallest climb 4 tiles. Widest gap at the same height 7 tiles, 6 when landing 2 or 3 higher,
    8 when landing 1 or 2 lower, 9 when landing 3 or more lower.
  - Run speed 3.25 px/tick (8 tiles/s). Platforms 1.6 px/tick (0.25 s per tile).
  - Holding jump re-jumps on landing. Coyote time 5 ticks.
  - `stepUp` 14px, which lets players climb out of pools and onto slope tops. Downhill snap 8px.
  - Every level was re-spaced for these numbers (section 4). Changing `PHYS` again means
    re-checking all 32 with the gate; the bot cache keys on `PHYS`, so it re-searches.
- **Collisions:**
  - Players and crates are AABBs.
  - The X pass resolves the leading tile column with step-up, then slopes under the entity's
    centre, then dynamic solids. Crates get pushed, recursively up to depth 3.
    A crate pinned against stone is kicked 28 px clear when a player who leaned on it for 20
    ticks jumps into its upper corner (`tryKick` in `shared/sim.js`); never off a plate.
  - The Y pass is crossing-based: it only collides when the previous bottom was above the
    surface, so an entity that is already overlapping is ignored.
  - Slopes are sampled at the entity's centre column with a 6px tolerance.
  - Players pass through each other.
- **Moving platforms:** each has two positions (`x0,y0` "off" and `x1,y1` "on") and moves at
  `platSpeed` px/tick.
  - Riders are carried through `gref` (the id of what an entity stands on). Platform ids are
    their index; crates are `100+i`; players are `200+i`.
  - If a platform would crush something, it reverts its move that tick ("blocked").
  - Initial position accounts for `inv` and for levers that start on.
- **Mechanism groups** (0-5), each with a colour: purple, green, orange, white, pink, yellow.
  - A group is active if ANY plate in it is pressed OR ANY lever in it is on (OR logic).
  - Plates are pressed by any living player or crate whose bottom is on the floor and which
    overlaps the plate's inner area.
  - Levers are pushed by entities moving faster than 0.3px/tick through the lever's tile. They
    move 0.22 per tick and **spring to whichever side they were pushed past centre**.
  - `on = angle > 0`, meaning pushed to the right.
- **Phases:** `PH_PLAY`, `PH_DEAD` (auto-resets the whole level after `DEATH_TICKS=80`, timer
  included, and increments `tries`), `PH_WON` (both doors fully open).
- **`rankFor`:** A = all gems and under par; B = one of the two; C = neither.
- **Hooks for the versus modes**, all additive: `tileAt` asks `L.tileFn` first (the runner's
  endless world), `mkPlayer`, `moveY`, `moveEnt`, `stepPlayer` and `checkHazards` are exported,
  and Dodgeball adds `IN_ACT = 8` and `stepPlayersOnly()`.

### Netcode

- **Server** (`server/index.js`):
  - Each room runs `step()` at 60 Hz using queued inputs per character.
  - It broadcasts `{t:'s', ack:[seqSol,seqLuna], inp:[bitsSol,bitsLuna], st:<full state>}` every
    2 ticks (30 Hz).
  - If a player's input queue runs dry, their last input is held for 10 ticks, then released.
  - A queue longer than 12 is trimmed.
- **Client** (`main.js`):
  - Predicts every tick with its own input and the last known remote input, and sends `{t:'i', s, b}`.
  - On each snapshot it takes the server state, replays its own unacked inputs, and carries the
    visual difference as a decaying offset (`app.off`), so there's no visible snap.
  - The partner is predicted from their last known keys, so a jump heard one round trip late
    leaves a 60-180 px error. Their offset is carried whole (up to 240 px) and paid back at most
    6 px per tick; your own character keeps the tight 64 px / 0.8 decay. Same in the Runner
    (`runner-app.js`). Measured Nov 17 with `tools/playtest/online-browser.mjs` and
    `runner-online.mjs`: the drawn partner moves at most 13 px per frame at `LAG_MS=80` and 150
    (was 108-133 px), and your own character's backward correction stays 0 px.
  - It interpolates between ticks for high-refresh displays.
- **Messages:**

  | Direction | Messages |
  | --- | --- |
  | Client → server | `create`, `join{code}`, `swap{char?}`, `start{level}` or `start{mode}` (`'dodge'`, `'runner'`), `restart`, `menu`, `leave`, `ping` (either player may send any of them) |
  | Server → client | `room`, `start{level, mode, st}` (runner: `start{mode, seed, st}`), `s`, `menu`, `peer-left`, `err`, `pong` |
- **Characters:** the host starts as Sol and a guest gets the free character. Either player can
  click a character in the lobby, press "Swap characters", or swap from the pause menu (that
  restarts the level with the new roles). Fixed Nov 2026: with one player in the room, swap used
  to flip the character twice and do nothing, and it was ignored once a level had started.
- **Testing aids:**
  - `LAG_MS=80 npm start` adds artificial one-way latency on the server.
  - `window.__app` is exposed in the browser for Playwright tests (harmless; keep or remove).

### Rendering

- Everything is drawn as vector shapes at `devicePixelRatio`, so it stays sharp at any size.
- The background and stone are cached once per level/resize in `buildStatic()`. Liquids, gems,
  doors, mechanisms, characters and particles draw every frame.
- Sound and particles are triggered by observing state changes, which keeps them robust to rollback.
- A step-up (out of a pool, onto a step) is drawn as a short ease (`app.lift` in `main.js`), not
  a one-frame 14 px pop. Rendering only.
- The start hint is placed per room at run time (`placeHint()`), away from players, doors and
  mechanisms. `node tools/playtest/hint-overlap.mjs` checks all 32 levels.
- The HUD is drawn on the canvas; the menus are DOM.
- The title screen runs a demo of level 1 behind the menu.

### Progress

- Saved in `localStorage` under `sl.progress.v1` as `{unlocked, best: {levelIdx: {t, gems, rank}}}`.
- `?unlock` in the URL unlocks all levels.
- Online, the host's progress decides which levels can be picked.

## 4. Levels

The full brief, the measured engine rules and the design rules are in `docs/level-plan.md`.
Read it before building or changing a level. This section is the short version.

### How a level is built

- Each level is a Python function in `tools/acts/act1.py` .. `act6.py`, registered with
  `@level(n)`. Its docstring holds the solution chain, one numbered step per cooperative move.
  The DSL is `tools/lvdsl.py`; level 1 in `act1.py` is the worked example.
- Each level has a bot script in the matching `tools/acts/actN.bot.js`. The bot is the proof that
  the level can be won.
- `python3 tools/levelgen.py` writes `shared/levels.js`. `--show 7` also prints level 7 with
  rulers. A level whose function raises keeps its last good build (`tools/acts/.last/`).
- Coordinates are tiles, x 1..38, y 1..28. The outer ring is always wall.

**Map legend:** `#` stone, `/` `\` ramps, `S` `M` `X` sun/moon/shade pools, `a`/`b` spawns,
`A`/`B` doors, `s`/`m` gems, `k` crate, `1-6` plate for group 0-5, `! @ $ % ^ &` lever for
group 0-5, `= - _ + ~ :` platform cells for group 0-5 (moves given via `mv(g, dx, dy)`).

**DSL helpers** (methods on `Lv`; most return the level, so they chain):

| Helper | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `r` / `clear` | Fill / empty a rectangle. |
| `set(x, y, c)` | One cell. Chains. |
| `floor`, `pool`, `rampR`, `rampL`, `basin`, `p` | Floor row, pool row, ramps with stone under them, a sunk pool with ramps, a string of map characters. |
| `plate(x, y, g)`, `lever(x, y, g, on=False)` | Controls. `on=True` starts a lever on. |
| `mv(g, dx, dy)` | Set a group's platform movement. |
| `gate(x, y0, y1, ch, g, slot_top)` | Vertical gate that rises into a stone pillar slot. Sets the group's move to (0, -h) and warns on stderr if that overwrites a different move for the group. |
| `floorgate(x, ytop, h, g)` | Gate that sinks into a slot in a thick floor. Clears its slot. |
| `plat(x, y, w, h, dx, dy, g, inv)` | Explicit platform, for several directions per group. It does not clear stone under it or along its path; clear that yourself. |
| `invert(g)` | Run a group's platforms the other way. |

**Platform index order matters** (scripts reference `S.pl[i]`): drawn platform cells come first,
in row-major scan order of their top-left cell, then explicit `plat()` / `floorgate()` entries.

### Re-spacing for the lower gravity (Nov 17, 2026)

With the higher jump, a climb or crossing under a low roof bonks and loses height. A full jump
needs 6 free rows above the take-off and along the arc. So every level was re-spaced:

- Stacked floors go 7 rows apart: four bands on floors 28, 21, 14 and 7, or staggered columns
  on 28/21/14/7 and 25/18/11/4.
- Rows that nobody jumps in can stay low (2 or 3 rows): walk-only corridors, wading tunnels,
  character-only pools under a 2-row roof.
- What blocks has grown: a climb of 5+, a gap of 9+ at the same height, an open pool 9+ wide.
- The gate fails a level if any climb or crossing on the bot's route hits a roof.

### The gate

A level is done when `node tools/gate.js <n>` passes in full. It checks:

- LAYOUT: stone fills 25-65%, no empty block over 120 cells, enough distinct floor heights over
  20+ rows, no flat stone floor over 18, at most one near-full-width floor, 21+ of 24 sectors with floor.
- DEPTH: plates and levers, gem counts by tier, bot time and control uses above the tier floor,
  par within 1.3-1.8 times the bot time (each level is set to about 1.5 times, so rank A is within reach of a human pair).
- CO-OP: the bot wins and collects every gem.
- ROUTE: sectors visited by both players and by each one.
- ROOF: 0 climbs or crossings on the bot route hit a roof. `node tools/check-headroom.js <n>` lists them.
- Solo check: neither player reaches their door alone. `FAST=1` skips it.

Things to know about it:

- The bot caches searches in `tools/acts/.botcache`, keyed on the level and `PHYS`, so a passing
  script replays in seconds. `NOCACHE=1` forces a fresh search.
- `seek()` caches by the goal's source text. A helper that builds goals from its arguments must
  pass `{ args }` (or `key`), or two different goals share one cache entry.
- Each search has a 90 s wall-clock limit. On a loaded machine a gate can fail with "gave up
  after the time limit". Re-run that level alone before you change anything.
- The solo check on a crate level can take 800 MB. Run full gates one or two at a time.

## 5. Verification

```bash
python3 tools/levelgen.py                # regenerate shared/levels.js from tools/acts/
python3 tools/levelgen.py --show 7       # ... and print level 7 with rulers
node tools/gate.js 7                     # the full gate for one level (PASS = done)
FAST=1 node tools/gate.js 2,3,8          # the gate without the solo check
node tools/gate.js                       # every level (slow: one solo check per level)
node tools/check-headroom.js 3,8         # route jumps that hit a roof
V=1 node tools/coop-bot.js 7             # play level 7 with the bot, printing every phase
NOCACHE=1 node tools/coop-bot.js 7       # same, ignoring cached searches
node tools/check-solo.js 1,2,3           # solo search to 400k states (1.5M on crate levels)
HELP=none node tools/check-levels.js 4   # the same search, with gem counts; TRACE=1 prints a path
node tools/calibrate.js                  # movement envelope
node tools/check-dodge.js                # Dodgeball rules, ~5 s
node tools/check-runner.js               # Runner fairness proof, 50 seeds to 1700 tiles, ~5 min on 6 jobs
```

- **Levels.** On Nov 17, 2026 the full gate, solo check included, passed all 32 levels with a
  fresh bot cache and 0 roof hits on every bot route. Level 29's solo check alone takes about
  27 minutes. Margins at or near a limit are in `docs/level-plan.md` under "Status and thin margins".
- **The bot.** `coop-bot.js` plays every level with two scripted players. The search phases
  (`goto`, `grab`, `press`, `flip`, `pushTo`, `seek`, `walk`) find inputs with the real physics;
  hand phases (`wait`, `mv`, `push`, `hop`, `hopTo`, `jumpWhen`, `leap`, `both`) cover moments that
  need both players at once or exact timing. The top of `tools/botlib.js` documents them.
  - Gotchas: when walking through a lever, the target must be past the lever tile, or the bot
    overshoots, walks back and reverts the lever. Keep players out from under descending lifts.
  - A search that fails with "gave up after the time limit" on a busy machine usually passes
    when run alone.
- **Solo check.** The gate searches 300k states per character (500k on crate levels);
  `check-solo.js` searches 400k (1.5M). A pass is a bounded search, not a proof: a solo route
  deeper than the limit can't be ruled out. On Nov 17 the gate's search hit its limit without a
  solo finish in 12, 14, 15, 19 and 25.
- **Everything at once.** `./tools/check-all.sh` runs the gate on every level, then
  `check-dodge.js` and `check-runner.js`. `LEVELS=1,5` limits the gate and `FAST=1` skips its
  solo check. The old `check-deps.js`, which asserted dependency tables for first-pass rooms that
  no longer exist, was deleted on Nov 17, 2026 (it is in git history).
- **Dodgeball and Runner.** See sections 9 and 10.
- **Online:** tested with two Playwright contexts: host, join by code, play, compare positions.
  Nov 2026: two `ws` clients swapped characters (alone, in the lobby and mid-level) and won
  level 1 online through the server; the lobby UI was checked in two browser tabs.
  - Prediction tested at 160 ms round-trip with `LAG_MS=80`: no backward snaps on the local player.
- **Playwright:** pre-installed in the sandbox (`require('playwright')`, `chromium.launch()`).
  Start a server on any port, e.g. `PORT=3123 node server/index.js`.

**Not verified:**
- Human playtesting of the rebuilt rooms and of the new gravity.
- Whether a first-time pair can read each room's links. The bot never has to work anything out.

## 9. Dodgeball (versus mode, Nov 2026)

A competitive mode on one single-screen court, Sol on the left and Luna on the right.

- **Code.** Rules, court and ball live in `shared/dodge.js` (deterministic, used by browser and
  server). `sim.js` only gained `IN_ACT = 8` and `stepPlayersOnly()`; co-op levels are unchanged.
  Client: `public/js/dodge-draw.js` (veil, fireball / moonball, trails, effects; hooked from
  `render.js`) and `public/js/dodge-ui.js` (HUD, sounds). `main.js` treats the level `'dodge'`
  like a level index (`levelOf`, `newState`, `stepAny`). The server's `Room` has a `mode`;
  clients send `{t:'start', mode:'dodge'}`.
- **Controls.** Hold the action key (Sol `↓`, Luna `S`, pad X/B/RT, touch `●`) to aim, release to
  throw toward the other side. A tap is flat; holding swings the aim arrow up and down.
- **Rules.** A thrown ball is live until it touches a floor or platform top, or a wall or ceiling
  for the second time (one bank shot counts). A live ball touching the other player scores. A dead
  ball belongs to whoever's side it is on; touching it picks it up. 6 s shot clock per possession,
  then the ball goes over; a throw that stays on your side doesn't reset it. The player who was
  hit serves next. First to 5. The first serve comes from a seeded RNG kept in the state.
- **Tuning** (`DODGE` in `shared/dodge.js`): ball 24 px/tick. Net to far back wall (457 px) takes
  0.30 s; half-centre to half-centre 0.32 s. A flat throw needs only 3 ticks of jump to clear,
  so a jump at 0.2 s reaction dodges only from the back third of the court and a 0.25 s jump only
  from the back wall. Closer than about 340 px you have to read the throw.
- **Checks.** `node tools/check-dodge.js` (~5 s): speed, dodge window table, settling bound and
  reachability, possession, scoring, determinism. `tools/check-dodge-browser.cjs` drives local
  and online play in Chrome and writes screenshots (see `docs/img/dodgeball/`).
- **Known issue.** Online, the defender sees a remote throw about one round trip late (at 100 ms
  RTT a spawn-to-spawn throw is visible for ~4 frames instead of 14). The drawn ball catches up
  smoothly, but dodging online is harder than local. Options: slow the ball by measured RTT per
  room, or rewind the victim on the server (lag compensation).

## 10. Runner (versus mode, Nov 2026)

An endless, seeded side-scroller for two: the view scrolls right and speeds up, the last player
standing wins. Local (one keyboard) and online rooms (server authoritative, client prediction).
Everything is in `docs/runner.md`: files, the hooks in the shell files, the difficulty ramp, the
obstacles and how each meets the fairness rules, and the checks.

```bash
node tools/check-runner.js                  # fairness proof, 50 seeds to 1700 tiles, real sim
JOBS=6 PARTS=4 node tools/check-runner.js   # explicit worker threads and work units per seed
SEEDS=11-20 TILES=600 node tools/check-runner.js   # a quicker subset
PORT=3217 node server/index.js & PORT=3217 OUT=docs/img/runner node tools/runner-browser.js
```

- Keep `stepRunner` free of `Math.sin`/`cos` (prediction must match the server). Swings use a
  polynomial.
- `sim.js` changes for it are additive: `tileAt` checks `L.tileFn`, and five internals are exported.
- The checker runs seeds in parallel worker threads (`JOBS`, default CPU count minus one, at most 8)
  and splits each seed's chunk tests into `PARTS` units (default 3). The 50-seed, 1700-tile proof
  is clean and took 308 s on 6 jobs on a busy machine.

A 3D Runner is being built on another branch. Once merged, it is documented in `docs/runner3d.md`.
